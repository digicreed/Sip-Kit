Right — it has sip_ua, but that is WebRTC. sip_ua (dart-sip-ua) is a port of JsSIP: it does SIP signaling only over WebSocket and media only over WebRTC (DTLS-SRTP). It's not a general SIP stack.

Proof from the file I just read, webrtc_engine.dart:79-81:

final settings = UaSettings()
  ..webSocketUrl = config.wsUrl          // the ONLY transport input — a wss:// URL
  ..webSocketSettings.allowBadCertificate = true

UaSettings has no host/port, no UDP, no TCP, no TLS-SIP option — the single entry point is webSocketUrl. And the media it produces is WebRTC MediaStream (lines 4, 250-253). So sip_ua requires:

your PBX to expose a WSS endpoint (Asterisk transport-wss on :8089/ws), and
WebRTC-enabled endpoints (DTLS-SRTP, ICE, rtcp_mux, etc.).

It cannot register to a plain SIP UDP:5060/TCP/TLS endpoint, and it can't do plain RTP. Having it in the box is exactly the path you're trying to avoid — it doesn't rescue you from WebRTC, it's the definition of it.

So whether it "solves your issue" depends entirely on what's forcing "not WebRTC":

If the goal is just "make/receive calls to your Asterisk from Flutter" and you're fine enabling WSS+WebRTC on the PBX (your repo's pjsip.conf already shows a transport-wss) → then yes, sip_ua/WebrtcEngine works today, zero native code. "Not via WebRTC" isn't actually a requirement in that case.
If you specifically need classic SIP — talking to a carrier/PBX that only offers plain SIP over UDP/TCP/TLS, or a policy of not turning on WebRTC media — → then no, sip_ua can't do it, and you're back to finishing the stubbed PjsipEngine (real PJSUA2 binaries + the ~13 handlers).

What's driving the "not WebRTC" constraint — is your Asterisk unable/unwilling to do WSS+WebRTC, or is it a different SIP target (a carrier trunk, a legacy PBX) that only speaks plain SIP? That decides whether you ship sip_ua as-is or invest in native PJSIP.
